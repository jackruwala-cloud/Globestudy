# Duration of Status Ends: Fixed Admission Periods for F-1 Students (DHS Final Rule, effective Sept 15, 2026)

## What is changing
On July 17, 2026, the U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS) published a final rule, "Establishing a Fixed Time Period of Admission and an Extension of Stay Procedure for Nonimmigrant Academic Students, Exchange Visitors, and Representatives of Foreign Information Media." It replaces the long-standing "duration of status" (D/S) framework for F (academic student), J (exchange visitor), and I (foreign media) nonimmigrants with a fixed, time-limited period of admission. The rule's effective date is September 15, 2026.

## What "duration of status" meant (the old rule)
Under duration of status, an F-1 student was admitted for as long as they maintained status in their program, with no single fixed end date on the admission, plus a 60-day grace period after completing the program or authorized practical training.

## The new fixed admission period
Under the final rule, an F-1 student applying for admission or a change of status to F is admitted only until the program end date listed on the Form I-20 — not to exceed 4 years — followed by a 30-day grace period after the program end date (shorter than the previous 60 days). Students who need more time than their fixed admission period must apply for a formal Extension of Stay (EOS) through U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS), rather than simply continuing under D/S.

## Important caveats
This rule was classified as a "major rule" and is subject to Congressional review, so details could still change before or after the effective date. Whether and exactly how it applies to a given student (including students already in the United States on duration of status) depends on the final regulatory text and USCIS/SEVP guidance. Because this is a new, high-impact change to how long you may lawfully remain, confirm your specific situation with your Designated School Official (DSO) / international student office and, where appropriate, an immigration attorney. Do not rely on older "duration of status / 60-day grace" guidance without checking whether the new rule applies to you.
