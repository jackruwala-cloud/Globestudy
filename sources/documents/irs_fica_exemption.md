# FICA (Social Security & Medicare) Tax Exemption for F-1 and J-1 Students

## Who is exempt from FICA
Nonresident aliens temporarily present in the United States in F-1, J-1, M-1, or Q-1/Q-2 nonimmigrant status are exempt from Social Security and Medicare (FICA) taxes on wages paid to them for services performed to carry out the purpose for which they were admitted to the United States. This exemption is provided by the Internal Revenue Code, section 3121(b)(19). The exemption applies only while the individual is a NONRESIDENT ALIEN for U.S. tax purposes.

## The exemption ends when you become a resident alien
The FICA exemption is tied to nonresident status, not to the visa type by itself. Once an F-1 or J-1 student becomes a resident alien for tax purposes (generally by meeting the Substantial Presence Test after their exempt-individual period ends), wages become subject to Social Security and Medicare taxes in the same way as for a U.S. citizen. F-1 and J-1 students are generally treated as "exempt individuals" (who do not count days of presence toward the Substantial Presence Test) for the first 5 calendar years in that status. After that 5-year period, they begin counting days and can become resident aliens.

## OPT and CPT wages
The exemption covers services allowed by the student's nonimmigrant status. For F-1 students this includes authorized on-campus employment, Curricular Practical Training (CPT), and Optional Practical Training (OPT), including the STEM OPT extension. Therefore, wages earned while working on OPT or CPT are exempt from FICA taxes AS LONG AS the student is still a nonresident alien for tax purposes. If an employer withholds Social Security or Medicare tax in error, the student should first request a refund from the employer; if the employer cannot refund it, the student can claim it from the IRS using Form 843 and Form 8316.

## What FICA exemption does NOT mean
Exemption from FICA (Social Security and Medicare) does NOT mean exemption from federal or state income tax. Wages may still be subject to federal income tax withholding and reporting, and the student may still need to file a federal tax return (for example Form 1040-NR for nonresident aliens).
