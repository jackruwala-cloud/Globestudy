# Form 1042-S — Foreign Person's U.S. Source Income Subject to Withholding

## What Form 1042-S reports
Form 1042-S reports amounts paid to foreign persons (including nonresident alien students) that are U.S.-source income subject to withholding, and any tax withheld on that income. A withholding agent (such as a university or employer) files it with the IRS and gives a copy to the recipient. It is roughly the nonresident-alien counterpart to information returns like the W-2 or 1099, but for income subject to nonresident withholding rules.

## Income a student might see on a 1042-S
Common examples for international students include: taxable scholarship or fellowship grants that exceed tuition and required fees (for example amounts covering room, board, or a stipend); wages that are exempt from normal withholding under a U.S. income tax treaty; and certain other U.S.-source payments. Note that a treaty-exempt portion of wages is often reported on Form 1042-S rather than (or in addition to) a Form W-2.

## Why it matters for your tax return
If you receive a Form 1042-S, the income and any withholding shown on it generally must be reported on your U.S. tax return (typically Form 1040-NR for a nonresident alien). The withholding shown can often be credited against your tax or refunded if too much was withheld. Because a student may receive BOTH a W-2 and a 1042-S in the same year, and because treaty and scholarship rules are technical, this is a high-stakes filing question — confirm the correct treatment with a qualified tax professional or your school's international student/tax office.

## Timing
Withholding agents are generally required to furnish Form 1042-S to recipients by mid-March (around March 15) for the prior tax year. If you expect one and have not received it, contact the withholding agent (your school's payroll or international office) before filing.
