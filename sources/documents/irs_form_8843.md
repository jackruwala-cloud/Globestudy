# Form 8843 — Statement for Exempt Individuals (F-1 / J-1 Students)

## What Form 8843 is
Form 8843, "Statement for Exempt Individuals and Individuals With a Medical Condition," is used by alien individuals to explain the basis of their claim to exclude days of presence in the United States for purposes of the Substantial Presence Test. Students, teachers, and trainees use it to document days that do not count toward the test.

## Who must file
An F-1, J-1, M, or Q student who is a nonresident alien must file Form 8843 to explain the days excluded as an exempt individual. This includes students who had NO U.S. income during the year. In other words, even if you earned nothing and owe no tax, you are still generally required to file Form 8843 for each year you are an exempt individual. Each family member in exempt status (including F-2 / J-2 dependents) files a separate Form 8843.

## How it is filed
If you must also file a federal income tax return (for example Form 1040-NR because you had taxable U.S. income), attach Form 8843 to that return and file by the return's due date. If you do NOT have to file an income tax return, mail Form 8843 by itself to the address given in the form instructions, by the filing deadline (generally the same April deadline).

## Common student mistake
Many international students wrongly assume that no income means nothing to file. Form 8843 is an informational statement, not an income tax return, and is still required to preserve your exempt-individual (nonresident) status. Missing it does not usually create tax owed, but it can complicate your residency determination. Confirm your obligation with your school's international student office.
