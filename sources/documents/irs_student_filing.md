# Do International Students Have to File a U.S. Tax Return? Which Form to File

## If you had a job or any U.S. income (wages / on-campus job / W-2)
If you are a nonresident alien F-1 student and you received U.S. wages — for example from an on-campus job, CPT, or OPT — you generally must file a federal income tax return for that year to report the income. Nonresident aliens file Form 1040-NR (U.S. Nonresident Alien Income Tax Return), not the Form 1040 that residents and citizens use. Your employer reports your wages to you on Form W-2, and you use it to complete Form 1040-NR. There is no minimum dollar amount of income that triggers the filing requirement for a nonresident alien student — even a small amount of taxable wages generally means you must file.

## If you had NO U.S. income
Even with no U.S. income, an F-1 student who is a nonresident alien is still generally required to file Form 8843 for the year. Form 8843 is an informational statement, not an income tax return, and does not by itself create tax owed.

## Scholarships, fellowships, and treaty-exempt income
Filing is also required if you had a taxable scholarship or fellowship grant (generally amounts beyond tuition and required fees), income that is partly or fully exempt under a tax treaty, or any other income taxable under U.S. tax law. These amounts are often reported to you on Form 1042-S and must be reported on your Form 1040-NR.

## Which return, and by when
Nonresident alien students file Form 1040-NR (attaching Form 8843). The federal filing deadline is generally mid-April for the prior tax year. F/J/M/Q students are treated as engaged in a U.S. trade or business, which affects how the income is taxed on the 1040-NR.

## Where to get help filing
Many schools provide international students access to nonresident tax-preparation software (such as Sprintax) and host tax workshops through the international student office. The IRS-sponsored VITA program offers free help at some sites. Because your exact filing requirement depends on your income, residency status, and any treaty, confirm your specifics with your school's international student/tax office or a tax professional experienced with nonresident returns before filing.
